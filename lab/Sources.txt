The catalog covers 11 explicitly defined reaction pairs. All stock solutions are 0.100 mol/L. Amounts in mmol = volume in mL × concentration in mol/L. Theoretical precipitate mass in mg = amount in mmol × molar mass in g/mol. The model assumes complete stoichiometric conversion and neglects Ksp, activity, kinetics, solubility, dissolved CO2 and contamination. It is an educational approximation, not guaranteed laboratory prediction.

For zinc and aluminium hydroxides, additional OH dissolves precipitate according to the written hydroxo-complex stoichiometry. Zinc requires 2 additional OH per Zn; aluminium 1 per Al. Kinetic order and transient local pH are not modeled.

Carbonate with insufficient acid is explicitly unsupported because bicarbonate formation and gas partitioning need an equilibrium model. Unknown pairs are not labeled as no-reaction.

pH is computed only for HCl/NaOH, ideal strong acid/base, at 25 °C with pKw=14. Indicator visualization is phenolphthalein: transition approximately pH 8.2–10.0. No pH is invented for salt solutions.

Theoretical CO2 gas volume = mmol × 24.47 mL at 25 °C and 1 atm using an ideal gas model, not a measured collected volume.

Colors, transparency, particle size, gas bubbles, settling and pouring animation are schematic. Iron(II) oxidation, AgCl photochemistry, heating, temperature change and other subsequent reactions are not simulated. Filtration separates the modeled precipitate only; it does not change calculated chemistry. No chemical is safe to handle merely because it is shown here. Physical experiments require trained supervision and proper safety assessment.

Models: Original authored glassware of revolution, textured bench, stock bottles and filter visualization. CC0 model searches were blocked; no external models were included. Three.js is reused from periodic/vendor/ under its bundled MIT license. No runtime third-party 3D assets are fetched.
